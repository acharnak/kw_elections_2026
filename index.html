    How the research scores were assigned · all nine criteria and anchors

    Research assessment: Each whole-number 0–5 score is a review of the published program, relevant experience and documented delivery using the same anchors for everyone. It is a coarse judgment with a cited rationale and limits. It does not measure policy desirability, personal ability or future performance, and it is not a voting recommendation.

    Unknown means material was unavailable or insufficiently reviewed. A zero requires reviewed material with no support for that criterion; it never means missing evidence was silently scored zero. Experience and past delivery are assessed separately. Future promises to gather data count as a mechanism or accountability commitment, not as existing empirical support.

      My weights, total formula and unassessed criteria

      These default weights are the user’s stated preferences, not candidate assessments. Edit them to match your priorities. Enter percentage points (for example, 20), with a total of 100.

      Complete score = sum(score × weight) ÷ 5

      A score of 5 on every criterion gives 100. A score of 3 on every criterion gives 60. Zero is valid. A blank or unassessed positive-weight criterion stays unknown.

      Partial range: lower = points from known criteria; upper = lower + weights of unknown criteria. Coverage is the percentage of fixed weight assessed. For example, 38 known points and 15% unassessed weight give 38–53 / 100 with 85% coverage. The range represents unassessed criteria, not a confidence interval or probability. Unknowns are neither imputed nor renormalized.

      Your edit can deliberately leave a criterion unknown. “Use research” restores its original assessed value. Only complete totals can be sorted numerically; partial and unknown candidates stay in surname order below them.
